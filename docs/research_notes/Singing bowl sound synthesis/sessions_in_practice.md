# How singing bowl sessions are played in practice

What practitioner guides and sellers say a bowl session consists of, gathered on 2026-10-10 to decide whether sensory-sound should offer a session mode. There is no standard; the sources are mostly guides written by people who sell bowls or courses, and they are read here for what practitioners do, not for what they claim it does. The evidence on effects is in `effects_and_safety.md` and summarised at the end.

## The set

- Crystal (quartz) bowls are manufactured to pitch and sold as seven-bowl "chakra sets", one bowl per note C D E F G A B, in both 440 Hz and 432 Hz tunings ([Meinl](https://www.scan.co.uk/products/meinl-7-piece-crystal-singing-bowl-chakra-set-c-d-e-f-g-a-b-csbsetcha), [Thomann](https://www.thomann.co.uk/thomann_crystal_bowl_fc_set_432hz_6_12.htm), [Sunreed](https://sunreed.com/products/new-product-launches/8-14-7-note-432hz-perfect-pitch-empyrean-crystal-singing-bowl-set-set666)). The lowest note is the largest bowl: C at 12 to 14 inches down to B at 6 to 8 inches in full-size sets; handheld sets are 5 to 6.5 inches. One seller describes a chakra set as "musically a C major scale, in the 3rd octave" ([Ohm Store](https://www.theohmstore.co/products/7-bowl-curated-chakra-set)).
- Himalayan bronze bowls are hand-hammered and not tuned to exact notes. Sellers measure finished bowls and sort them into sets by nearest note, sometimes out of a hundred bowls at a time, and some also choose by how the bowls sound together; even "432 Hz" bronze sets are sold without a guarantee of pitch ([Sunreed](https://sunreed.com/collections/favorites-in-practice/products/8-bowl-432hz-himalayan-metal-singing-bowl-chakra-set-112), [Ohm Store](https://www.theohmstore.co/products/7-bowl-curated-chakra-set)). A bronze "set" is therefore an approximate scale, each bowl a little off, which matches the measured recordings in `recording_analysis.md`.
- Smaller sets are built from intervals: a second bowl a fifth above the first, a third bowl adding the major third, so C, G and E; sellers say the bowls must be closely matched in tuning for intervals to blend ([Sunreed, building a set](https://sunreed.com/blogs/articles/building-a-crystal-singing-bowl-set-over-time)).

## Order, arc and pace

- The common convention is to open by striking each bowl from the lowest to the highest ("root to crown") and to close by striking them softly in the reverse order ([Rainbow Sounds](https://www.rainbowsounds.co/blogs/rainbow-sounds-blog/sound-bath-tips), [Crystal CVNC](https://crystalcvnc.com/blogs/news/crystal-singing-bowl-placement-order)).
- Sessions run from 30 minutes to 2 hours, most often 45 to 90 minutes, with a settling period, a long middle and a quiet close ([Soundcy](https://soundcy.com/article/how-long-is-a-sound-bath), [Pure Spa Direct](https://purespadirect.com/blogs/pure-spa-direct-blog/the-step-by-step-protocol-for-a-sound-bath-therapy-session-ndash-your-ultimate-guide-to-sonic-relaxation)).
- The most detailed guide found ([Yoga Sound Bath](https://www.yogasoundbath.com/blog/playing-the-crystal-singing-bowls-a-complete-guide-to-designing-and-leading-a-sound-bath)) gives a five-phase arc, arrival, descent, depth, expansion, return, and a sample 60-minute timing of about 10, 15, 15, 12 and 8 minutes (its two sample timings disagree with each other). It introduces one bowl at a time at the start, letting each fade completely; limits the sounding bowls to three "unless the space is large or heavily sound-absorptive"; alternates strikes and rubbing "to avoid auditory fatigue"; and gives pause lengths: 5 to 10 s between tones in the same register, 10 to 20 s when moving from low to high, 30 to 60 s between phases, and 60 to 90 s of silence after the final tone, which is one clear strike of the lowest C. Short sessions of 20 to 30 minutes give 1 to 3 minutes per phase.
- Combinations: octaves and perfect fifths for opening and closing, triads in the middle (C E G, D F A, F A C), higher intervals for the expansion phase, dissonant clusters used sparingly and resolved. Two bowls close in pitch beat against each other and the guide treats that as a fault to correct by separating them or swapping one for a note a third or fifth away.
- Other instruments (gong, chimes, drum, rattle, rainstick) are placed before or after the bowls, with a warning against layering them over bowl sequences. A session of bowls alone is ordinary.

## What this means for a session mode

Most of a practitioner's session is sequencing, not a new sound: a tuned set (exact for crystal, approximate for bronze), an order, an arc with pauses, a limit on overlap, consonant combinations, and silence at the end. The pacing in these guides, one bowl at a time, long fades, few overlaps, silence to close, is closer to the design limits in `effects_and_safety.md` for sound-sensitive listeners than a random layer is. A session mode can reproduce what practitioners do without adopting the chakra labels or any claim about what the notes do.

## The claims, for the record

The chakra assignments (C for the root, up to B for the crown), the 432 Hz tuning and the solfeggio frequencies are repeated on nearly every page about bowls and supported by none of them; the pages cite no primary research ([Discovery UK](https://www.discoveryuk.com/?p=23456027), [Aura](https://webflow.aurahealth.io/blog/is-there-any-science-behind-solfeggio-frequencies)). The one widely cited study of bowl meditation, Goldsby et al. 2017, is observational, before and after a session with no control group ([record](https://doaj.org/article/bf90469a705d4041ab700d2ae0dc9501)). The fuller review is in `effects_and_safety.md`. A 432 Hz option in the library would be there because the instruments are sold both ways, not because the tuning does anything.
